### 2. Concept
Sensations are the physiological basis of perception. They work somewhat like sensors registering incoming data. _Perception_ is the process by which the brain selects, organizes, and interprets these sensations. In this sense, perception can be compared to computer code: it provides a structure through which data is processed, producing an output that can, in turn, be sensed and perceived.

The process from sensation to perception takes place within a single subject. When a subject wants to communicate its perception to the outside world, or to another species or community, that perception needs to be translated into a form the receiver can recognize or understand. Let us call this form an _outward expression_.

The James Webb Space Telescope offers one way of thinking about this process. Our exploration and perception of the universe depend heavily on external instruments and scientific methods. Data transmitted by Webb takes approximately five seconds to reach Earth. It arrives as bits rather than finished images, and these bits are subsequently processed into grayscale images.

NASA has also described the processing of Webb’s images as a human-centered process, because the infrared light Webb detects must be mapped onto visible colors that our eyes can perceive. The universe and human perception belong to different systems. To perceive the universe, we rely on complex methods that transform information into forms accessible to our senses and perceptual processes.

We find these nebulae beautiful, yet Webb’s raw images often appear almost entirely black when first opened. This does not mean they contain no information. Instead, the brightness values need to be stretched or rescaled to reveal the information captured through particular filters. What we might call the celestial bodies’ “outward expression” therefore requires translation before it becomes information we can perceive. Only then can we respond with an exclamation such as, “The stars are so beautiful!”

Thomas Thwaites’s _GoatMan_ project offers another example. Why adopt goat-like features and behaviors in an attempt to become part of a herd? Thwaites’s approach can be understood as an attempt to translate the outward expression of his perception into forms and behaviors that goats could recognize. This translation opens up the possibility of recognition and acceptance across species.

Once translated, this outward expression becomes “raw data” for another subject. For example, someone who does not understand English might encounter written English much as someone else encounters binary code: they can see the signs without understanding their meaning. The receiving subject senses and perceives the expression, beginning another process of sensation and perception within themselves.

My project, _My Creature_, explores and makes this process tangible. The creature’s movements and sounds are outward expressions of translated perception. Through them, I seek to understand the process of translation and the curiosity that draws one individual toward another. Every individual, within every species or community, perceives differently. The project explores how these different ways of perceiving might be expressed, encountered, and interpreted.![[Screenshot 2026-10-21 at 03.12.57.png]]
### 3. Format & technical considerations

I want to reassemble parts from different toys into a creature with a recognizable, nonhuman form—something resembling a spider or an octopus, for example.

This “creature” will use sensors to detect aspects of its surroundings, such as sound, light levels, and the number of people nearby. It will then translate these inputs—what I conceptualize as its “perception”—into movement, language, and poetry, creating outward expressions that humans can sense and interpret.

### 6. what is your plan for making

My making process will have three main stages.

First, I will collect the components, assemble the basic structure, and develop the programming and physical computing elements. The aim is to create a rough prototype that can move and produce variations in sound, with a physical form that begins to resemble a living creature.

For the WIP presentation, I will show my research, initial sketches, and the partially assembled prototype. For the viva, I aim to have the inflatable wall and sound-producing components working together, with the overall framework in place.

After that, I will refine the creature’s physical details, fine-tune its movements and sounds through programming, and develop the poetic qualities of the piece.
